What LGU INVENT does

A General Services Office has to know what the municipality owns, who is accountable for it, what is about to run out and what the auditors will ask for. LGU INVENT keeps all of that in one place. It tracks Property, Plant & Equipment (PPE) and Semi-Expendable (SE) items, assigns them to offices and people, and carries each purchase from request to delivery.

It was written for the General Services Office of a Philippine municipality, with the forms and reports the Commission on Audit (COA) expects: PAR, ICS, RPCPPE and RPCSE.

Purpose

  • Track PPE and Semi-Expendable property items.
  • Assign property to offices and personnel, with Property Acknowledgment Receipts (PAR) and Inventory Custody Slips (ICS).
  • Handle returns, transfers and unserviceable items.
  • Generate COA-compliant reports.
  • Manage the procurement pipeline: PR, RFQ, PO and AIR.
  • Forecast inventory demand with machine learning.
  • Keep a complete audit trail.

Who it is for. The GSO administrator, department staff, property custodians and auditors. Each gets a view of the data that matches the job, and nobody sees more than their role allows.

Problem statement and objectives

A municipal General Services Office has to account for every item the local government owns, and for every item it is about to buy. When property lists, custody forms, purchase requests and audit reports live in separate files, it is hard to say who holds what, what is running low, and whether the numbers will match what COA counts. Few local systems join custody, procurement, forecasting and COA-format reporting in one tool.

General objective

Develop a web-based property inventory management system that tracks PPE and semi-expendable property, supports PAR and ICS issuance and the procurement pipeline, forecasts demand, and produces COA-compliant reports for the Municipality of Lumban, Laguna.

Specific objectives

  • Record and classify property items as PPE or semi-expendable, with property tags and bulk import from Excel.
  • Issue property through PAR and ICS, and handle returns, transfers and unserviceable items with a chain of custody.
  • Carry each purchase through PR, RFQ, PO and AIR, and encode delivered items into the inventory.
  • Predict three-month demand and recommend reorder quantities with a gradient boosting model.
  • Generate RPCPPE, RPCSE and physical count reports, with role-based access and a complete audit trail.

Methodology

Design and data

The system keeps its records in a MySQL database of users, offices, property items, issuance records and items, purchase requests and their lines, consumable items with stock movements, notifications, user activity and monthly demand history. It follows an MVC pattern in PHP, with a JSON API between the browser and the database.

Forecasting pipeline

  1. Collect. Monthly demand history for each office and category.
  2. Build features. Time index, month, lag 1, lag 3 and a three-month moving average.
  3. Train. Gradient boosting with up to 16 decision stumps and a learning rate of 0.2, fitted on the first 80% of the history.
  4. Validate. Mean absolute error on the last 20%, compared against the moving-average baseline.
  5. Predict. Demand for the next three months, cached for the dashboard.
  6. Recommend. Apply the order formula and the stockout, reorder, watch, overstock and sufficient rules.

Request flow

Each request passes Apache URL rewriting, the router, a session check and a permission check. The page then calls the API, which reads or writes the database through PDO and returns JSON for the interface to render.

    From request to registered property

    Two flows carry most of the work. Issuance puts property in someone’s custody. Procurement brings new items in.

      Demand forecasting

      The forecasting module predicts monthly demand three months ahead for each office and category, then turns the prediction into a reorder suggestion. The model is gradient boosting built from decision stumps, written in PHP, with a three-month moving average as the baseline.

      In the current release, the Forecast page works from completed consumable purchase requests. It predicts next month’s spending and number of purchase requests for each department, and the page lists a briefing per office against its allotted budget.

      Sufficient

      Order qty = predicted demand + safety stock (1.25 × last-3-month average, at least 1) − on hand − incoming. The status checks follow the documented rules. This demo uses 120% of the reorder point for “near” and twice the 3-month demand for “high stock”, which are illustrative thresholds.

      System design

      PresentationHTML5, vanilla JavaScript, CSS3 and Bootstrap 5. A single-page admin app and a separate portal for staff.
      ApplicationPHP 7.4+ in an MVC pattern, with controllers, models, views and helpers.
      APIJSON endpoints in api/*.php, session-based authentication and CSRF protection.
      DataMySQL 5.7+ or MariaDB 10.2+, accessed through PDO with prepared statements. Database lgu_inventory, utf8mb4.

      Security

      • Session management and a login-protection mechanism.
      • Password hashing and permission checks on every request.
      • Prepared statements against SQL injection, output escaping against XSS, CSRF tokens.
      • Validated file uploads and audit logging of user actions.
